History

    What is BMI?

    Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple number that relates your weight to your height. It is widely used by doctors and health organisations as a quick screening tool to flag whether someone's weight may be low, typical or high for their height. It is a starting point for a conversation, not a diagnosis.

    The BMI formula

    BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m)]²

    This calculator takes height in centimetres and converts it to metres for you. For example, a person weighing 70 kg who is 175 cm tall: height = 1.75 m, so BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 ≈ 22.9.

    BMI categories

    BMICategory
    Below 18.5Underweight
    18.5 – 24.9Normal weight
    25 – 29.9Overweight
    30 and aboveObese

    These are the standard World Health Organization adult ranges. Some health bodies recommend lower cut-offs for South Asian populations, because health risks can begin at lower BMI values, so ask your doctor which thresholds apply to you.

    Worked examples

    Example 1

    58 kg and 168 cm: 58 ÷ (1.68 × 1.68) = 58 ÷ 2.8224 ≈ 20.5, which is in the normal range.

    Example 2

    92 kg and 170 cm: 92 ÷ 2.89 ≈ 31.8, which falls in the obese category.

    Limitations of BMI

    • It does not distinguish muscle from fat, so a muscular athlete can show a high BMI while being healthy.
    • It ignores where fat is stored. Waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio add important information.
    • It is designed for adults. Children and teenagers are assessed using age and sex-specific growth charts.
    • Age, pregnancy and certain medical conditions can change how the number should be read.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a healthy BMI?

    For most adults, 18.5 to 24.9 is the range considered normal by the WHO.

    How accurate is BMI?

    It is a reasonable population-level screening tool but less reliable for individuals. Use it alongside other measures.

    Should I change my diet based on this result?

    This site provides general information only. Speak to a doctor or registered dietitian before making health decisions.
