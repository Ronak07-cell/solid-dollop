    How the percentage calculator works

    This calculator answers the most common percentage question: what is X% of Y? Enter the percentage and the number, and it multiplies them for you. Percentages show up everywhere, including discounts in shops, exam marks, tax, tips, interest rates and statistics, and a quick way to get the answer saves time and avoids slips with decimals.

    The percentage formula

    A percentage is a fraction out of 100. The core formula is:

    Result = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number

    For example, 15% of 240 is (15 ÷ 100) × 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36.

    Worked examples

    Example 1: A shop discount

    A jacket costs ₹2,500 and is on sale for 20% off. The discount is 20% of 2,500 = 0.20 × 2,500 = ₹500, so you pay ₹2,000.

    Example 2: Exam marks

    You scored 72 out of 90 and want it as a percentage. Use the reverse form: (72 ÷ 90) × 100 = 80%.

    Example 3: Adding GST or a tip

    To add 18% GST to ₹1,200, calculate 18% of 1,200 = ₹216, then add it: ₹1,200 + ₹216 = ₹1,416. A 10% tip on a ₹850 bill is 0.10 × 850 = ₹85.

    Other useful percentage formulas

    • What percent is A of B? (A ÷ B) × 100
    • Percentage increase: ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100
    • Percentage decrease: ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100
    • Find the original before a discount: Sale price ÷ (1 − discount rate)

    Common mistakes

    • Forgetting to divide by 100, so 15% is entered as 15 instead of 0.15 in a manual calculation.
    • Mixing up the base. A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the starting value, because the second change applies to a larger number.
    • Confusing percentage points with percent. Going from 10% to 12% is a rise of 2 percentage points, but a 20% relative increase.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I calculate 5% of a number quickly?

    Find 10% by moving the decimal point one place left, then halve it. For 5% of 340: 10% is 34, half of that is 17.

    Can a percentage be more than 100?

    Yes. 150% of 40 is 60. Values above 100% simply mean more than the whole, which is common in growth figures.

    Is my data stored anywhere?

    No. Calculations run in your browser and your history stays on your own device.
